<div id="describe-text">
	<p style="text-align:justify">Hi there! I'm Ningjing Tang, a fourth-year PhD student in the <strong><a href="https://www.hcii.cmu.edu/">Human-Computer Interaction Institute</a></strong> at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by <strong><a href="https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/hongs/">Hong Shen</a></strong> and <strong><a href="https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~hheidari/">Hoda Heidari</a></strong>.</p>
	<p style="text-align:justify">I study <strong>AI safety</strong>, focusing on <strong>mental health risks</strong> in human–AI interactions. I work with affected communities to understand AI-related harms (e.g., differing user and clinical perspectives on delusion-related harms) and co-design AI safeguards (e.g., how and when AI should refuse in high-risk mental health support settings). I aim to develop human-centered AI safety evaluations informed by these perspectives. </p>
	<p style="text-align:justify">Prior to joining CMU, I obtained my bachelor's degree in Computer Science from Tsinghua University, where I was part of Xinya College, a residential liberal arts program.</p>
